## Set calories from the goal first

Most recomposition plans start near maintenance or in a modest calorie deficit. The exact target depends on starting body fat, training history, and how consistently the client can execute.

An aggressive calorie deficit can make the scale move faster, but it can also hurt lifting performance and recovery. If the goal is to look better, the plan has to protect or build muscle while fat comes down. To get an evidence-based daily calorie target and protein goal tailored to your body fat percentage and training history, use the [body recomposition calculator](/tools/body-recomposition-calculator).

That is why the first macro decision is the phase: cut, recomp, or lean bulk. The calorie target should match that decision.

When choosing your calorie starting point for body recomposition:
- **Maintenance Calories:** Eating at maintenance provides sufficient energy to support intense lifting sessions and muscle recovery while allowing your body to draw from stored fat to fuel daily activity. This approach works especially well for men who are relatively new to structured lifting or returning after a layoff.
- **Moderate Calorie Deficit:** Consuming a slight deficit—typically 200 to 400 calories below maintenance—accelerates fat reduction while maintaining enough energy to preserve lean tissue. This is the preferred route when body fat around the waist is the main limiting factor.

Slashing calories too aggressively remains the most common pitfall. When you drop food intake too low, your strength in the gym drops, recovery slows down, and the risk of losing lean muscle increases. A sustainable recomposition requires fueling your workouts while letting high protein and consistent lifting drive tissue remodeling.

## Protein is the anchor

Protein should be set before carbs and fats. It supports muscle retention, satiety, and recovery when calories are controlled.

For many men, a simple high-protein target is easier to execute than a complex meal plan. The useful plan is the one that can survive work, travel, restaurants, and weekends.

When calories are restricted or held at maintenance, protein provides the essential amino acids required for muscle protein synthesis. It also carries the highest thermic effect of any macronutrient and keeps hunger manageable across long workdays.

Once protein is stable, the remaining calories can be split between carbs and fats based on lifting performance and food preference. Locking in your daily protein target first gives you the flexibility to adapt carb and fat amounts to your schedule without compromising muscle preservation.

## What clinical evidence shows about recomposition macros

For years, standard gym dogma held that building muscle while losing fat was physiologically impossible for trained lifters. Conventional wisdom claimed that muscle hypertrophy required a caloric surplus, while fat loss required a calorie deficit.

Clinical research directly contradicts this rigid assumption. In a randomized controlled trial published in the *European Journal of Applied Physiology*, Vargas-Molina and colleagues (2026) evaluated body recomposition in resistance-trained participants across different nutritional protocols (PMID 41940947).

The researchers compared three distinct groups over a 10-week intervention:
1. **Moderate Energy Deficit Group (DEF):** Followed a moderate calorie deficit protocol combined with an elevated protein intake of 2.5 g·kg⁻¹·d⁻¹ alongside structured lifting.
2. **Maintenance Calorie Group (ISO):** Followed an isocaloric maintenance protocol with the identical elevated protein intake of 2.5 g·kg⁻¹·d⁻¹ alongside structured lifting.
3. **Control Group:** Followed habitual dietary practices without structured nutritional intervention while completing the same lifting program.

The measured outcomes confirmed that simultaneous muscle gain and fat loss occurred in both structured nutrition groups:
- **Fat Loss:** The moderate energy deficit group reduced fat mass by 2.94 kg (and fat-free adipose tissue by 0.47 kg). The maintenance group also achieved fat loss, decreasing fat mass by 1.41 kg (and fat-free adipose tissue by 0.25 kg).
- **Muscle Gain:** Fat-free mass increased in both nutritional groups. The moderate deficit group gained 1.04 kg of fat-free mass (1.50 kg when adjusted for fat-free adipose tissue). The maintenance group gained 0.97 kg of fat-free mass (1.22 kg when adjusted for fat-free adipose tissue).
- **The Control Difference:** The control group following habitual practices without nutritional targets showed no meaningful changes across any body composition measurement.

The time and group analysis demonstrated a reliable difference for fat-free mass and adjusted fat-free mass between the structured nutrition groups and the control group.

These findings challenge the traditional model of energy balance by proving that resistance-trained individuals can achieve body recomposition when an elevated protein intake (2.5 g·kg⁻¹·d⁻¹) is paired with structured lifting. Whether eating in a moderate deficit or at maintenance calories, structured protein intake enabled participants to build lean muscle while simultaneously reducing body fat.

## How to set carbs and fats

After locking in your daily calorie ceiling and anchoring protein, you distribute the remaining calories between carbohydrates and dietary fats.

Neither carbs nor fats should be eliminated. Both macronutrients serve distinct physiological functions that directly influence training output and adherence:

### 1. Set a Dietary Fat Baseline
Dietary fats are essential for hormone production, fat-soluble vitamin absorption, and cell membrane integrity. Dropping fats too low can lead to hormonal disruption, low energy, and poor diet satisfaction. A sensible fat baseline typically sits between 0.3 and 0.45 grams per pound of bodyweight (or roughly 20% to 30% of total daily calories). Prioritize quality fat sources such as olive oil, avocados, nuts, whole eggs, and fatty fish.

### 2. Allocate Carbohydrates to Fuel Lifting
Once protein and fat requirements are satisfied, allocate your remaining daily calories to carbohydrates. Carbohydrates are the primary fuel source for high-intensity lifting sessions and replenish muscle glycogen stores. Adequate carb intake supports training volume, maintains workout intensity, and helps prevent fatigue during demanding sessions. Focus on complex sources like oats, rice, potatoes, quinoa, and fruit around your training windows.

### 3. Balance Based on Preference and Schedule
The exact split between carbs and fats can flex depending on personal preference and workout timing. Some men feel more energized and perform better with a higher-carbohydrate approach, while others prefer slightly more dietary fat for meal satiety. As long as total calories and protein remain locked in, you have the flexibility to adjust carb and fat ratios to match your lifestyle.

## Adjust from weekly data

A recomposition macro plan should not be frozen for months. Waist, photos, bodyweight trend, gym performance, hunger, and adherence tell you whether to adjust.

If weight and waist are not moving and adherence is real, calories may need to come down. If performance and recovery are falling apart, the plan may be too aggressive.

Tracking multiple data points prevents premature or emotional adjustments:
- **Weekly Bodyweight Averages:** Weigh yourself 3 to 4 mornings per week under identical conditions and track the rolling weekly average. Day-to-day fluctuations in water, sodium, and digestion are normal and should not trigger diet changes.
- **Waist Circumference:** Measure your waist at the navel once per week. If your waist is steadily decreasing while scale weight is relatively flat and gym strength is climbing, recomposition is succeeding.
- **Progress Photos:** Compare standardized photos every 3 to 4 weeks. Visual changes in muscle definition often appear before significant movements on the scale.
- **Gym Performance Logs:** Track weights and repetitions on primary compound movements. Maintaining or increasing strength confirms that your macros are supporting muscle retention and recovery.

Coaching keeps the decision tied to data instead of emotion after one bad weigh-in. When adjustments are necessary, make small modifications—such as adjusting daily intake by 100 to 200 calories—and monitor the trend for two to three weeks before making further changes.

## Honest limits

When applying the scientific evidence on body recomposition macros, consider these practical boundaries:

- **Participant Demographics:** The trial by Vargas-Molina and colleagues (2026, PMID 41940947) studied young, resistance-trained individuals. Advanced lifters who are already very lean may experience slower rates of recomposition compared to newer lifters or individuals with higher starting body fat.
- **Elevated Protein Protocol:** The study evaluated an elevated protein intake of 2.5 g·kg⁻¹·d⁻¹. While this confirmed that elevated protein facilitates simultaneous fat reduction and muscle gain, the study did not compare this directly against lower protein thresholds.
- **Nutritional Structure Is Essential:** In the Vargas-Molina et al. (2026) investigation, the control group completed the exact same lifting program but followed habitual eating, resulting in no meaningful changes. Lifting alone without controlling calorie balance and daily protein is insufficient to drive recomposition.
- **Time Horizon:** The clinical intervention lasted 10 weeks. Real-world physique changes require consistent adherence over several months to achieve substantial transformations.

## FAQ

### What are the best macros for body recomposition?

Start with a calorie target that matches the phase (either maintenance or a moderate deficit), set protein high enough to support muscle retention, keep fats at a reasonable baseline for hormonal health, and use carbohydrates to support lifting performance and recovery.

### Should I eat at maintenance for body recomposition?

Some people can recomp near maintenance, especially newer or returning lifters who can build muscle efficiently while burning fat for energy. Others benefit from a moderate calorie deficit if reducing abdominal fat is the main limiting factor.

### Can I use a macro calculator for body recomposition?

Yes. A calculator is a useful starting point to establish your baseline calorie and protein targets, but weekly data from waist measurements, progress photos, and gym performance should decide whether your targets need to move.

### How much protein is needed for body recomposition?

In the clinical investigation by Vargas-Molina and colleagues (2026, PMID 41940947), resistance-trained participants consumed an elevated protein intake of 2.5 g·kg⁻¹·d⁻¹. This high protein baseline supported simultaneous muscle gain and fat loss across both moderate deficit and maintenance protocols.
